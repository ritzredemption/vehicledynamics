How it works

All you need is a car and its wheels

1 · The vehicle body

One mesh for the car body, facing forward.

2 · The wheels, separated

Each wheel as its own mesh, with its pivot at the centre.

Add the four wheels to the Blueprint and press Play. The plugin handles suspension, tires, engine and brakes.

Step 2 · Prepare the mesh

Five rules for any car model

  • 1
    +X forward, +Z up

    Nose along +X. Apply all transforms before export.

  • 2
    Real-world size

    1 m in the DCC tool = 100 Unreal units. Check the wheelbase.

  • 3
    Body origin on the ground

    Centred between the axles, all body parts merged into one mesh.

  • 4
    One mesh per wheel

    Pivot at the hub, rolling axis along Y, hub facing outward.

  • 5
    Simple collision hull

    A UCX_ convex hull whose bottom sits about 30 cm above the ground.

SUV sample: body raised, wheels pulled out, orange = UCX collision hull.

Gamepad and Blueprint

Right / left trigger and left stick work out of the box. For AI or RL agents, disable the debug driver and call SetInputs() each step.

Weather

Wet roads, rain and tire marks

Grip that reacts to water

Wet asphalt lowers friction through its Physical Material. The tire model responds automatically: longer braking, earlier slides.

Spray from the tread

Water is thrown up and back in proportion to speed, from a fixed droplet pool.

Tracks and skid marks

Rolling tires cut matte tracks through standing water. Spinning or locked wheels leave rubber.

Rain on the GPU

Falling streaks are animated in the material, so rain adds almost no CPU cost.

Add a Vehicle Surface FX component to the car and a VD Rain actor to the level, or run BuildWetTrack.py
